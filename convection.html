Cooling a hot plate with moving air

Change the wind, the heating or the air temperature and watch the plate find a new balance.

    Computed from the equations

    • Air velocity and pressure: the 2D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations on a 100 × 48 grid, advanced with a projection method. The pressure equation is solved exactly every step, so mass is conserved to round-off.
    • Air temperature: the advection-diffusion energy equation on the same grid, with a flux limiter so it cannot overshoot.
    • Plate temperature: heat conduction along the aluminum, fed by the heater and drained by the air above it.
    • Every number in the panels above, including the energy balance, which closes on its own once the plate settles.
    • The time step, recalculated each step from the Courant and diffusion limits.

    Drawn for the eye

    • Colours are blended smoothly between grid cells. The solver itself has one temperature per cell.
    • Tracers and arrows follow the computed velocity, but tracers move at a slowed, steady pace so you can follow them. They are not part of the solution.
    • The tunnel is drawn twice as tall as it is, and the plate thicker than its 3 mm.
    • The ÷1000 heat capacity setting changes how fast the plate warms, not where it ends up. Choose real aluminum to see the true warm-up, which takes about half an hour of simulated time.

    How far to trust it

    With conduction in the plate switched off, the solver's average heat transfer coefficient lands within 3% of the textbook laminar result for an evenly heated plate, at every speed from 0.3 to 2 m/s. With the aluminum switched on, the plate spreads heat sideways so well that it ends up nearly one temperature, and the coefficient follows the even-temperature curve instead. That is the real behaviour of a thick conductive plate, and you can see it in the chart on the right.

    What it leaves out: buoyancy, radiation, turbulence, the third dimension, and the change of air properties as it warms. Properties are taken at the inlet temperature. The derivation, the numerical method and a worked case are on the math page.
