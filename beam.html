    Computed from the model

    • The stiffness matrix: one 4 × 4 matrix per element, assembled into a global matrix with two unknowns per node, displacement and rotation.
    • The fixed end: the wall node's displacement and rotation are removed from the system, which is what stops the matrix being singular.
    • The solution of K u = F by Gaussian elimination with pivoting, rerun on every change.
    • Bending moment and stress, recovered from the solved displacements through the element shape functions.
    • The wall reactions and the residual, which check the solution against the original, unreduced matrix.

    Drawn for the eye

    • The bent shape is the solved displacement multiplied by the magnification shown above the beam. At 1× it is true scale, and usually too small to see.
    • The curve between nodes uses the same cubic shape functions the elements use, so it is the model's own answer, not a smoothed sketch.
    • Cross-sections are drawn square to the bent centreline, which is the Euler-Bernoulli assumption.
    • Very thin beams are drawn thicker than scale so the stress colours stay visible. A label says when.

    Why the error is essentially zero

    For a single load at the free end, the exact deflected shape is a cubic curve. Euler-Bernoulli elements describe the beam with cubic curves, so they reproduce the exact answer with any number of elements, even one. The tiny difference you see is floating-point round-off, and it grows slightly with more elements because the matrix gets larger. More elements would matter for loads spread along the beam, which this model does not include.

    The model assumes the material stays elastic, deflections stay small next to the length, and the beam is slender enough that shear can be ignored. The warnings above tell you when one of those stops being true. The full derivation with numbers is on the math page.
